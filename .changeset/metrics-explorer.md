Explore has a new **Metrics** page next to Logs, Errors and Traces. It lists every metric your services send in the time range, with its type, unit, data point count and when it last reported, and you can search by name or filter by type. Open a metric to chart it: gauges show avg, min, max or sum, counters show a per-second rate or the increase per interval (cumulative counters stay correct across process restarts), UpDownCounters show the sum across series, and histograms show p50, p95 or p99 (worked out from bucket counts), the average, or the observation rate, plus a heatmap of observations per bucket over time. When the series in one line report different bucket bounds, for example two services on different SDK versions, they are combined on one set of bounds and a note says whether the percentiles are exact or estimated. When your SDK records exemplars, the chart marks the slowest sampled request in each interval with a diamond; click one to open its trace or its logs. A chart is never finer than how often the metric is exported, so a metric sent every 15 minutes draws a line rather than scattered points, and the header shows the chart's resolution. Add up to three more metrics to the same chart, each with its own aggregation, grouping and filters, hide any of them from the chart without losing its setup, and click a metric's name to switch it to another. Group the chart by up to three attributes, filter by attribute values, and drag across the chart to zoom. Service and Environment carry over from the other Explore pages, and their menus on every Explore page now include services and environments that send only metrics. Exponential histograms chart the same way as histograms, and stay exact when an SDK changes their scale. Summaries chart the quantiles your SDK reports, the average and the rate; since an SDK computes a summary's quantiles per instance, a line that covers several instances shows the highest of them, and you can group by instance to see each one.
