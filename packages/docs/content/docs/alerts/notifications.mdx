---
title: Notifications
description: Send firing and resolved alerts to Email, Telegram, and Slack, with one notification per evaluation.
---

When an alert evaluates, Everr sends one notification summarizing all instance changes. Notifications are configured in the app, not as code, and apply across the whole organization.

## Open Notifications

In Everr, go to **Alerts** and click **Notifications** (top right of the Alerts page). The page has three layers: where alerts go by default, the custom rules that redirect specific alerts elsewhere, and an Advanced section for less common controls.

## Where alerts go

The top card lists your organization's default channels: Email, Telegram, and Slack. Each has an enable toggle and its recipients. An enabled channel must have at least one recipient before you can save.

### Email

Turn on **Email** and add one or more recipient addresses.

### Telegram

Turn on **Telegram**, then set a **bot token** (like `123456789:ABC...`) and one or more **chat IDs** (for example `-1001234567890` for a group). To create a bot and get its token, follow Telegram's [BotFather guide](https://core.telegram.org/bots/features#botfather).

The bot token is write-only: after saving, Everr never shows it again. To change it, clear the field and paste in a new one.

To find a chat ID, add your bot to the chat and read the ID from the Telegram Bot API [`getUpdates`](https://core.telegram.org/bots/api#getupdates) response, or open the channel or group in the [Telegram web app](https://web.telegram.org) and copy the number from the URL. For channels and supergroups it's the `-100...` value.

### Slack

Turn on **Slack** and paste an **Incoming Webhook** URL. In Slack, open the [Slack app dashboard](https://api.slack.com/apps), select the app that should post alerts, then go to **Incoming Webhooks**. Turn on **Activate Incoming Webhooks** if needed, click **Add New Webhook to Workspace**, choose the channel, and authorize the app. Copy the resulting `https://hooks.slack.com/services/...` URL and paste it into the field.

### Remind every

Choose how often a still-firing alert re-sends its notification: off, every hour, every 4 hours, or every 24 hours.

## Custom notification rules

By default every alert notifies the channels above. Add a custom notification rule to send specific alerts somewhere else instead: a condition (for example `severity = critical`), a target channel, and a priority. Rules are checked top to bottom by priority, and the first match wins.

## Advanced

The Advanced section is collapsed by default. Expand it for a pipeline overview and three less common controls: dependency mutes, the webhook feed, and read-only channels.

### Dependency mutes

Mute noisy downstream alerts while a related, higher-level alert is already firing. While alerts matching a source condition are firing, alerts matching a target condition are muted, optionally scoped so it only applies when both share the same value for chosen labels.

### Webhook feed

The fallback: alerts that match no custom notification rule are delivered to every webhook URL added here.

### Channels

The underlying delivery channels, with secret fields redacted. Add or delete channels directly in the UI; custom notification rules deliver to them by name. Channels are not defined as code: `kind: AlertRule` is the only alerting resource `everr apply` reconciles, and channels, custom notification rules, dependency mutes, and webhook feeds are all managed in the app.

## Mute during maintenance

To stop notifications for a known issue or maintenance window without deleting the rule, mute it from the alert's detail page or from the Alerts page. Use the **Mute** action on a firing instance and set a time window, or mute an entire rule from the mutes pill at the top of the Alerts page. The rule keeps evaluating, muted instances are filtered out of notifications. If every instance is muted, no notification is sent.
